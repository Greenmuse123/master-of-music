# 02 — Story Bible

**Project:** Master of Music
**Document version:** 1.0 (2026-05-11)

> The fresh session must treat this document as the canonical narrative source. Dialogue, side-quests, and incidental NPCs may be invented in development — but anything contradicting the lore, character voice, or plot beats here must be revised here first.

## 1. The premise in one paragraph

The universe of **Sonus** is a living symphony. Reality is held together by an underlying resonance called **the Great Unison** — a constant, multi-layered chord that flows through every mountain, river, instrument, and lung. As long as the Unison plays, the world coheres: weather follows tempo, seasons follow key changes, life follows melody. But the Unison is not automatic; it is *performed* — by every musician and instrument alive, knowingly or not, every moment they make sound. When all genres play together, the chord holds. When one genre tries to silence the others, the chord cracks. **That crack is The Discord, and it is spreading.**

Our hero is **Sol Reed**, a young jazz saxophonist from Jazz City, and his living tenor saxophone **Brass**, who together must travel across the seven musical kingdoms of Sonus, recruit defectors and rivals, and reunify the genres before the Unison falls silent forever.

## 2. The world: Sonus

### 2.1 Cosmology

- **The Great Unison** — a cosmic chord underlying all reality. Heard as a faint, always-on harmonic drone in quiet places. Cannot be heard inside the Discord's territory.
- **The First Composers** — mythic prehistoric beings (8 of them, one per "founding genre") who first played the Unison into existence. Their instruments still exist as artifacts: the **Founding Instruments**, scattered, lost, or hoarded.
- **The Resonance** — the felt energy of music in living beings. Drained by silence. Restored by play.

### 2.2 Geography (seven kingdoms + neutral hub)

| # | Region | Vibe | Reference | Combat texture |
|---|--------|------|-----------|----------------|
| 1 | **Jazz City** | Speakeasies, bridges over canals, neon, brass everywhere. Cosmopolitan. | New Orleans × 1940s NYC × Bioshock | Improvised; bonus to off-beat moves |
| 2 | **The Blues Bayou** | Swamps, lantern-lit shacks, slow-rolling rivers. Melancholy. | Mississippi Delta × Pirates of the Caribbean | Sustained notes; HP regen on minor key |
| 3 | **The Classical Conservatory** | Cliffside marble palaces, hedge mazes, observatories. Orderly. | Vienna × Bach × Hogwarts | Strict tempo; perfect timing required |
| 4 | **The Rock Citadel** | Volcanic spires, electrified gates, lightning storms. Defiant. | Mordor × Iron Maiden stage | High damage, low defense; power chords AoE |
| 5 | **The Hip-Hop Bloc** | Stacked metropolis, street-level energy, layered rooftops. Inventive. | NYC × Bronx × Akira | Combo chains; sample-and-loop mechanic |
| 6 | **The Electronic Wastes** | Crystalline desert, glitching landscape, neon ruins. Strange. | Tron × Dune × Geometry Wars | Procedural patterns; speed > damage |
| 7 | **The Folk Heartlands** | Rolling hills, mead halls, ancient trees. Communal. | Ireland × Tolkien Shire | Party-wide buffs; storytelling chains |
| H | **The Conservatorium** (hub) | Floating amphitheater at the center of Sonus. Where all genres meet — or used to. | Pantheon × Cloud District | Tutorial / safe zone / save point |

The Discord originated at the **Conservatorium** and is spreading outward in rings. By Act II, two regions are already silent.

### 2.3 In-world rules of magic

1. **Music = power.** Any sentient creature can produce Resonance by playing or singing. Resonance fuels every magical effect in the world.
2. **Genre = identity.** A creature is born into a genre and may learn others. Cross-genre play is rare, powerful, and politically suspect.
3. **Silence kills.** Sustained silence in an area collapses the local Unison; the area "forgets itself" and physically dissolves into the Discord.
4. **Instruments live.** Most are sentient and biological — they have lifespans, preferences, opinions. The "instrument-musician" relationship is more partnership than ownership.
5. **The Founding Instruments are special.** Each holds a piece of the original Unison chord. Reassembling them is the only way to restart the chord if it stops.

## 3. Cast

### 3.1 Protagonist

**SOL REED** — 19 years old. Tenor saxophonist. Born in Jazz City, raised by his grandmother, a former Conservatorium teacher who never explained why she left. Quiet, observant, more comfortable letting his sax speak. Reluctant hero archetype, but unlike many of the type he *wants* to play — he just doesn't want to lead. His arc is learning that lead and improvise are not opposites.

- **Visual:** Slim, dark coat, fingerless gloves, gold-rimmed shades that he only puts on for big moments. Designed to read at 32×48 silhouette.
- **Voice:** Few words. Plays a riff to answer most yes/no questions.
- **Combat:** Bridges all genres. Starts weak in everything; ends strong in everything if the player explores broadly.

### 3.2 Brass — the saxophone

**BRASS** — Sol's tenor sax. Older than he looks (probably). Sentient. Sarcastic. Knows more about the Discord than he's letting on, because he was *there* when it started.

- **Visual:** Polished brass body with subtle scratches; eyes appear in the bell during dialogue.
- **Voice:** Speaks in full sentences — the only "instrument" who does. Theatrical, slightly weary, paternal.
- **Combat:** Always equipped. Doubles as the player's UI guide and tutorial voice.
- **Mystery:** Brass once belonged to a First Composer. Reveal lands in Act III.

### 3.3 Recruitable party (MVP set: 3 of 8)

| Name | Instrument | Genre | Role | Personality |
|------|-----------|-------|------|-------------|
| **Velma "Vel" Mute** | Trumpet | Jazz | DPS / lead | Hot-headed, jealous of Sol, eventual best friend |
| **Old Pete "Walkin'"** | Upright bass | Blues | Tank / sustain | Gentle, slow, knows everyone |
| **Etude** | Violin | Classical | Healer / buffs | Snobbish at first, secretly a fan |

MVP must ship with **Vel, Pete, Etude** as recruitable. The other five are post-MVP.

### 3.4 Antagonists

**MAESTRO CACOPHONY** — the principal villain. Once **Maestro Cantabile**, head of the Conservatorium and Sol's grandmother's old colleague. Lost a child in an accident he blamed on dissonance. Now believes the only way to protect the world is to silence every genre except one — *his* — and conduct it himself, forever. He is not insane; he is grieving, and his solution will end the world.

- **Visual:** Towering, gaunt, baton-conductor silhouette, robe that flickers between musical staves and static.
- **Combat:** Final boss. Three phases corresponding to the three pillars.
- **Tragic angle:** He is right that disunity is dangerous. He is wrong that the answer is silence. The player should leave the final fight conflicted.

**THE QUARTET OF DISCORD** — four mid-boss generals, one per Act:
1. **Stacatta** — wields percussive silence; rules the Electronic Wastes.
2. **Fermata** — freezes notes mid-air; rules the Classical Conservatory after the coup.
3. **Diminuendo** — drains Resonance; haunts the Blues Bayou.
4. **Tutti Null** — a void choir; final pre-boss encounter at the Conservatorium gates.

Each Quartet member should feel like a complete Cuphead-grade encounter. See [`04-COMBAT_SYSTEM.md`](04-COMBAT_SYSTEM.md) §5.

## 4. Plot — three-act structure

### Prologue (15–30 min playtime)

Jazz City, midnight. Sol plays a rooftop set with Brass. Halfway through a solo, the Unison drops out — for the first time in his life, he hears the world without the chord. Down in the streets, a "silent zone" is spreading. His grandmother gives him a sealed envelope, refuses to explain, and pushes him onto a riverboat south. **Tutorial: movement, dialogue, first encounter (a streetlamp-creature turned hollow by the Discord).**

### Act I — The Bayou (3–4 hours)

Sol meets **Vel** (rival-trumpeter) on the boat and **Old Pete** at the bayou docks. They investigate the spreading silence. Encounter Quartet member **Diminuendo**. Recover the first **Founding Instrument** (a clay flute). End-of-act revelation: the Discord is being deliberately spread by an organization called the **Silent Order**.

### Act II — The Conservatory & The Wastes (5–6 hours)

Travel to the Classical Conservatory; find it under occupation by **Fermata**. Recruit **Etude**, who defects. Escape into the Electronic Wastes, find **Stacatta** waiting. Mid-act: Sol's grandmother dies offscreen — letter from her arrives explaining she was a former First-Composer adept. Recover the second and third Founding Instruments.

### Act III — The Citadel, The Heartlands, The Bloc (4–5 hours)

Diplomatic and combat arcs through Rock, Folk, and Hip-Hop kingdoms. The player must choose two of three to **fully unite** (the third gives a partial alliance); choice affects ending. Recover remaining Founding Instruments. **Tutti Null** is fought at the gates of the Conservatorium.

### Climax & Endings

At the silent center of the Conservatorium, Sol confronts **Maestro Cacophony**. Three-phase boss fight (combat, dialogue, duet). The final phase is not a combat fight — it is an improvised duet, and the player must respond to Cacophony's grief in their own playing.

**Three endings**, gated by player choices throughout:
- **Unity Ending** (all genres allied + duet succeeds): Unison restored, Cacophony surrenders, joins the new Conservatorium. Bittersweet, hopeful.
- **Sacrifice Ending** (partial unity + duet succeeds): Unison restored, but Brass is destroyed in the process. Sol becomes a teacher.
- **Silent Ending** (duet fails): Cacophony wins. Sol survives. The world is one-genre forever. A "play again" prompt offers the player a chance to undo.

## 5. Themes & motifs

- **Unity is plural, not uniform.** The villain wants one genre; the heroes win by keeping all of them.
- **Improvisation is a virtue.** Plans break; play continues.
- **Inheritance is not a debt.** Sol's grandmother left him a gift, not an obligation.
- **Silence is a person.** The villain isn't evil — he's grieving. Combat doesn't end the story; conversation does.

Recurring **motif**: the same five-note phrase appears throughout the score in different keys, instruments, and tempos — first as Sol's warmup riff, eventually as the Great Unison itself.

## 6. Dialogue style guide

- **Sol Reed:** ≤6 words per line. Often answers with a sax phrase (rendered as italicized music notation in the textbox).
- **Brass:** Theatrical, archaic ("Dear boy, the bass section disagrees"), one Shakespeare quote per chapter, never sentimental until Act III.
- **Vel:** Fast, sharp, slang-heavy, uses jazz vocab as everyday adjectives ("That guy's a flat seven").
- **Maestro Cacophony:** Long sentences, perfect grammar, terrifying calm. Never raises his voice. The one time he does, the player should flinch.
- **NPCs:** Speak in the dialect of their genre. Bayou NPCs drawl; Conservatory NPCs use formal verbs and Italian musical terms; Rock NPCs use lots of em-dashes.

## 7. Worldbuilding rules (for writers and AI-assisted dialogue)

1. **Never explain the Unison as "magic."** It's physics. People disagree about its mechanism, not its existence.
2. **No real-world musician names.** No Coltrane, no Hendrix. Use fictional analogues whose initials may pun (e.g., the legendary Jazz City founder is "Jonathan Coal").
3. **Instruments use first names with a nickname suffix.** "Old Pete Walkin'", "Vel Mute", "Brass" (he refuses a last name). Conservatory characters break the rule and use Italian-style stage names ("Etude").
4. **Sonus has no monotheism.** People swear by composers, not gods. "By the Eighth Composer's beat—"
5. **The word "song" is sacred.** People say "tune," "piece," "phrase," "set." A "song" is something only a First Composer plays. When an NPC says "song," pay attention.
6. **Death:** Characters who die in service of music become "echoes" — heard but not seen. Sol's grandmother becomes an echo and can guide him at one critical moment in Act III.
